LRU vs LFU — the same access pattern, side by side

Both caches hold 3 keys and receive an identical sequence. Watch what each one throws away when put D arrives — they disagree, and the reason they disagree is the difference between the two policies.

LRU — Least Recently Used

Evicts the key untouched for the longest. Recency only; it does not count hits.

LFU — Least Frequently Used

Evicts the least-used key; ties broken by recency. One DLL per frequency.

They evicted different keys.

Why a doubly linked list? Both policies need O(1) removal of an arbitrary node. The hash map gives you the node pointer in O(1); the prev link lets you splice it out without walking. Neither structure alone is enough.

Why LFU is O(1) and not O(log n): min_freq is never searched for. It resets to 1 on every insert, and only ever increments by 1 when a promotion empties its bucket. That single invariant is what removes the need for a heap — and it is where nearly every buggy LFU goes wrong.
